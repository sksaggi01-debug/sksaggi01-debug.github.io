Task authoring: a single-verifiable-answer problem built to break frontier models

The assignment type: write an original, self-contained economics task with one exact answer, concept tags, a step-by-step chain of reasoning, an answer-format field and a grading rubric. The task must be solvable by hand by a specialist but lead frontier models to a wrong final answer.

Answer typeExact integer, no units
Concept tags4
Reasoning chain6 steps, ends in \boxed{}
ComputationPen and paper; integers under 6 figures

1. The prompt

Molded resin kayak paddles are sold in a regional market. At a price of $40 buyers take 60 paddles a week, and at $70 they take 30; demand is linear. Every paddle uses 2 kg of resin at $3.50 per kg plus $3 of packaging, and nothing else varies with output. To operate in a given week a producer must pay a $140 mould lease and a $60 safety licence, whether or not it sells anything. Producers are identical, choose quantities simultaneously, and enter until one more entrant would earn negative profit. The number of producers must be a whole number. The state introduces an excise of $30 per paddle, collected from producers, and counts tax receipts as part of social welfare. After producers re-optimise and entry or exit takes place, by how much does weekly total surplus (consumer surplus plus producer profit plus tax receipts) fall?

Concept tags

  • Cournot oligopoly
  • Free entry with integer constraint
  • Excise tax incidence
  • Welfare accounting with fixed costs

Answer format

Give the fall in weekly total surplus as an exact integer with no units, currency symbol or thousands separator. Example format: 725

2. Chain of reasoning

Step 1 Demand from two points: slope = (70 − 40)/(30 − 60) = −1 → P = 100 − Q Step 2 Marginal cost: 2 × 3.50 + 3 = 10. Weekly fixed cost: 140 + 60 = 200 Step 3 Cournot with n firms and unit tax t: q = (90 − t)/(n + 1), profit = q² − 200 Step 4 No tax: q = 90/(n + 1). n = 5 gives 15² − 200 = 25 ≥ 0; n = 6 gives (90/7)² − 200 = −34.7 → n = 5, q = 15, Q = 75, P = 25 W₀ = CS 2,812.5 + profits 5 × 25 = 125 → 2,937.5 Step 5 Tax $30: q = 60/(n + 1). n = 3 gives 15² − 200 = 25 ≥ 0; n = 4 gives 12² − 200 = −56 → n = 3, q = 15, Q = 45, P = 55 W₁ = CS 1,012.5 + profits 3 × 25 = 75 + receipts 30 × 45 = 1,350 → 2,437.5 Step 6 ΔW = 2,937.5 − 2,437.5 = 500 \boxed{500}

3. Where models are designed to fail

Each trap is a known frontier-model habit. The table lists the answer each one produces.

Model answerFailure mode
688 (687.5)Holds the number of firms at 5 after the tax. Firm profit turns negative (−100 each) and the solver never re-runs the entry condition.
450Treats n as continuous (n = 90/√200 − 1 ≈ 5.36 before the tax, ≈ 3.24 after) instead of taking the largest whole number.
900Leaves fixed costs out of welfare, so the saving from two fewer mould leases disappears.
1,850Ignores tax receipts, counting the $1,350 transfer as a loss.

Design note: output per firm is 15 both before and after the tax, so a solver checking only firm-level quantities sees no change. The entire welfare effect runs through the number of firms, which is where the reasoning must go.

4. Grading rubric (10 points)

CriterionPointsFull credit requires
Primitives2P = 100 − Q derived from the two points; MC = 10 and fixed cost = 200 derived from the components.
Pre-tax entry2Checks n = 5 and n = 6 explicitly; reports q = 15, P = 25.
Post-tax entry2Re-applies the entry condition with the tax; checks n = 3 and n = 4; reports P = 55.
Welfare accounting3Includes consumer surplus, profits net of fixed costs, and tax receipts in both states.
Final answer1500, in the required integer format.
