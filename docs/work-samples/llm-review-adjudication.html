LLM response review and adjudication

Two assignment types. In an LLM response review, a model has critiqued an economics task and proposed an alternate version; the reviewer decides whether the critique is right, whether the original task stands, and whether the alternate is valid and better. In adjudication, the reviewer rules on other experts' verdicts for the same item, and a correct dissent outweighs a mistaken majority.

Part 1. LLM response review

Section 1 · Original task

A closed economy has C = 100 + 0.8(Y − T), T = 100, I = 200 − 20r and G = 150. Real money demand is L = 0.5Y − 25r and the real money supply is M/P = 500. Government purchases rise by 30. By how much does equilibrium output rise? Key: 50.

Section 2 · LLM-generated feedback

"The task is invalid. The price level P is never given, so the real money supply cannot be computed and the LM curve is undefined. Error modes: (1) missing price level; (2) solvers may assume P = 1 arbitrarily."

Section 3 · Alternate task

Same as Section 1, but the money market reads: "Money demand is L = 0.5Y − 25r. The nominal money supply is M = 1,000 and the price level is P = 2." Key unchanged: 50.

Does the LLM feedback make the original task invalid?

No — the feedback is incorrect, and the original task is valid as written

The task gives the real money supply directly, M/P = 500, so P is not needed. The LM curve is 500 = 0.5Y − 25r, or Y = 1,000 + 50r. The IS curve is Y = 5(220 + G) − 100r. With G = 150: r = 17/3, Y = 3,850/3. With G = 180: r = 20/3, Y = 4,000/3. Output rises by exactly 50, matching the key (multiplier 5/3 against a simple multiplier of 5, the gap being crowding out). Neither listed error mode exists: no price level is required and no solver has to assume P = 1.

Is the alternate task an improvement?

No

It fixes a problem that did not exist and adds one that did not. The original states money demand and money supply in the same real units. The alternate gives nominal M and P but leaves money demand unlabeled. A solver who reads L as nominal sets 1,000 = 0.5Y − 25r, and the equilibrium interest rate becomes −1 before the policy change, a negative rate the original task never produces.

Is the alternate task valid, and does it address all error modes in Section 2?

Yes, valid

The asked quantity survives the ambiguity. In this linear model the output change does not depend on the money supply. Read as real balances of 500, Y rises from 3,850/3 to 4,000/3; read as 1,000, Y rises from 1,950 to 2,000. Either way ΔY = 50, so the key is still uniquely correct and the listed error modes are addressed, though they were never real. I would still ask the author to label money demand as real, because any question on the level of r or Y would break.

Part 2. Adjudication

Item under adjudication (constructed)

A monopolist faces inverse demand P = 60 − 2Q and constant marginal cost 12. The government levies a tax of $8 per unit, collected from consumers. Which statement about the post-tax equilibrium is correct?

Options include C: "Consumers pay a gross price of $40 per unit" (key) and G: "The monopolist receives $32 per unit."

Reviewer verdicts

ReviewerVerdictNote
R1Accept"Solved: Q = 10, P = 40. Key correct."
R2Accept with edits"Correct; tighten wording of option E."
R3Needs revision"G is also true: the firm nets 40 − 8 = 32. Two correct options."

Adjudication

Needs revision — R3 upheld

Independent solution. The firm's net inverse demand is 52 − 2Q, so MR = 52 − 4Q = 12 gives Q = 10. Consumers pay 60 − 20 = 40; the firm receives 40 − 8 = 32. Before the tax, Q = 12 and P = 36, so consumers bear $4 of the $8 tax.

Ruling. Options C and G are both true statements about the same equilibrium, so the item has two correct answers and fails the single-best-answer standard. R1 and R2 verified the key but did not test the distractors. R3 is correct. The two-to-one majority does not change the outcome.

Feedback to reviewers. R1, R2: a correct key is necessary but not sufficient; every distractor must be checked for being true. R3: accurate and specific; flag for strong review. Instruction to author: change G to a false statement built on a real error, for example "The monopolist receives $36 per unit, because a tax collected from consumers does not affect the seller."
