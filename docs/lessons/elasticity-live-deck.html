    Part 1 · Price elasticity of demand

    Why we use the midpoint method

    A plain percentage change depends on which point you start from. The midpoint method divides by the average, so you get the same answer in both directions.

    Ed = %ΔQ%ΔP , where %ΔQ = Q2 − Q1(Q1 + Q2) ÷ 2 × 100

    %ΔP is calculated the same way using P1 and P2. We report the absolute value, because for demand the sign is always negative.

    Part 2 · Total revenue test

    Raise the price: do you gain more than you lose?

    Total revenue (TR) = P × Q, which is the area of the rectangle under the demand point. A price change adds one strip of revenue and removes another. Elasticity tells you which strip is bigger.

    Wrap-up

    The elasticity summary sheet

    MeasureMidpoint formulaHow to read it
    Price elasticity of demand|%ΔQd ÷ %ΔP|> 1 elastic · = 1 unit elastic · < 1 inelastic · 0 perfectly inelastic · ∞ perfectly elastic
    Price elasticity of supply%ΔQs ÷ %ΔPSame cutoffs as demand. Supply is usually more elastic in the long run than in the short run.
    Cross-price elasticity%ΔQx ÷ %ΔPy+ substitutes · − complements · 0 unrelated
    Income elasticity%ΔQx ÷ %ΔIncome− inferior · between 0 and 1 normal necessity · > 1 normal luxury

    Total revenue test

    Elastic: price and TR move in opposite directions. Inelastic: they move together. Unit elastic: TR does not change. With the midpoint method, this test always gives the right answer.

    Linear demand

    Above the midpoint, demand is elastic. At the midpoint, it is unit elastic and TR is at its maximum. Below the midpoint, it is inelastic. The slope is the same everywhere on the line, but elasticity is not.

    Linear supply

    Extend the supply line. If it meets the price axis, supply is elastic. If it passes through the origin, it is unit elastic. If it meets the quantity axis, it is inelastic.
