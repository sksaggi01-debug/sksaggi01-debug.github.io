    The one test that settles every item

    Movement

    The good's own price changed, and nothing else did. Buyers slide along a curve that stays put — downward when the price falls, upward when it rises. Report it as a change in quantity demanded.

    Shift

    Something other than the good's own price changed — income, price of a substitute or complement, tastes, expectations, number of buyers. The whole curve relocates: outward if buyers want more at every price, inward if they want less. Report it as a change in demand.

    Where the marks go. Three items (01, 06, 10) are movements; seven are shifts. Movement has exactly one cause — the good's own price — while a shift can come from any of six determinants, so the shifters get more practice.

    Item 10 is the one most often missed, and it is a wording error rather than a diagram error. Demand is the whole curve; quantity demanded is one point on it.

    Items 02 and 08 are a matched pair on income: same determinant, opposite direction, because one good is normal and the other inferior.

    All scenarios are hypothetical. Prices, quantities and percentages are constructed for classification practice and are not drawn from any published dataset.
