Evaluation method · original samples

How I build and check an expert economics item.

Three problems written for this page only. None is taken from, or modelled on, a client submission. Each shows the verified answer, the derivation, and the wrong answers a model is most likely to reach. All numbers were checked with exact arithmetic.

Sample 1 · Public economics

A harbour ferry sells 1,000 tickets a week at a fare of $20 and 600 at $30. Operators supply 200 tickets a week when they net $10 per ticket and 800 when they net $25. Both schedules are linear. The town already levies a $4 per-ticket tax on operators and proposes raising it to $8. What is the marginal excess burden of the increase, defined as the additional deadweight loss per additional dollar of tax revenue?

Answer: 3/14 ≈ 0.214

Derivation
Demand Q = 1800 − 40P Supply Q = 40P − 200 No tax P = 25, Q = 800 Tax t Pd = 25 + t/2, Q = 800 − 20t DWL(t) = ½ · t · 20t = 10t² Revenue R(t) = t(800 − 20t) t = 4 DWL = 160 R = 2,880 t = 8 DWL = 640 R = 5,120 MEB = (640 − 160) / (5,120 − 2,880) = 480 / 2,240 = 3/14 ≈ 0.214
Planted traps
ValueError that produces it
0.125Average excess burden at $8 (640 / 5,120), not the marginal burden of the increase.
0.167Mechanical revenue: $4 × 720 = 2,880, ignoring that the base shrinks to 640.
0.071Ignores the existing tax: prices the new $4 wedge as a fresh triangle (160 / 2,240). The rectangle between the old and new wedge is the part most often missed.
0.286Puts the total deadweight loss at $8 over the revenue change (640 / 2,240).

Sample 2 · Industrial organization

Inverse market demand is P = 120 − Q. Firm 1 has constant marginal cost 20 and firm 2 has constant marginal cost 30; neither has fixed costs. Compare simultaneous quantity choice (Cournot) with the case in which firm 1 commits to its quantity first and firm 2 observes it (Stackelberg, firm 1 leading). By how much does total surplus change?

Answer: rises by 27,775/72 ≈ 385.76

Derivation
Cournot q1 = 110/3, q2 = 80/3, Q = 190/3, P = 170/3 CS = 2,005.56 π1 = 1,344.44 π2 = 711.11 TS = 4,061.11 Follower q2 = (90 − q1)/2 Leader P = 75 − q1/2, π1 = (55 − q1/2)·q1 → q1 = 55 q2 = 17.5, Q = 72.5, P = 47.5 CS = 2,628.13 π1 = 1,512.50 π2 = 306.25 TS = 4,446.88 ΔTS = 4,446.875 − 4,061.111 = 27,775/72 ≈ 385.76

Two effects stack: total output rises, and output shifts toward the lower-cost firm. With equal costs of 25 the gain is only 219.36.

Planted traps
ValueError that produces it
+622.57Reports the change in consumer surplus only.
−236.81Reports the change in producer surplus only.
+168.06Reports the leader's profit gain.
+219.36Averages the costs to 25 and solves a symmetric game, losing the cost-reallocation effect.

Sample 3 · Macroeconomics, with a reviewer's flag

Output is Y = K1/3(AL)2/3. Population grows at 1%, technology at 2%, and capital depreciates at 5% a year. The saving rate is 20%. A planner moves the economy to the golden-rule saving rate. By what percentage does steady-state consumption per effective worker change?

Answer: rises by ≈ 7.58%

Derivation
k* = (s / (n + g + δ))^(3/2), y* = (s / (n + g + δ))^(1/2), c* = (1 − s)·y* s = 0.20 k* = 3.953 y* = 1.581 c* = 1.265 s = 1/3 (GR) k* = 8.505 y* = 2.041 c* = 1.361 c change = 1.361 / 1.265 − 1 = 7.58%
Planted traps
ValueError that produces it
+29.1%Compares output per effective worker instead of consumption.
+115.2%Compares capital per effective worker.
−23.9%Sets the golden-rule saving rate equal to labor's share (2/3) instead of capital's share.

Reviewer's flag: this item is weak as a benchmark question. The ratio cGR/c* = ((1 − α)/(1 − s))·(α/s)α/(1−α) does not depend on n, g or δ, so a solver who uses the wrong break-even investment rate (for example δ alone) still gets 7.58%. The growth-rate data are decorative. I would send it back and ask for a question about the level of consumption or the transition path, where n + g + δ matters.

Annotated review of a model response

A constructed response to Sample 1, scored the way I score model outputs: find the first incorrect step, name the error, and trace its effect on the final answer.

Model response (excerpt):

"Equilibrium is P = 25, Q = 800. With the $4 tax, quantity falls to 720, so DWL = ½ × 4 × 80 = 160. Raising the tax to $8 lowers quantity to 640, so DWL = ½ × 8 × 160 = 640, and the extra DWL is 480. Each extra dollar of tax is collected on the 720 tickets now sold, so extra revenue = 4 × 720 = 2,880. Marginal excess burden = 480 / 2,880 = 0.167."

Verdict: Incorrect final answer (0.167; correct value 0.214).

  • Correct steps: the demand and supply lines, the no-tax equilibrium, both quantities (720 and 640), and both deadweight losses (160 and 640).
  • First error: the revenue change. The response multiplies the $4 increase by the pre-increase base of 720. That charges the new $4 on 80 tickets that are no longer sold (+320) and ignores the $4 per ticket already collected on those 80 tickets, which is now lost (−320). The correct change is 8 × 640 − 4 × 720 = 2,240.
  • Effect: revenue gain is overstated by 640, so the marginal excess burden is understated by about 22% (0.167 vs 0.214).
  • Rubric note: the answer matches planted trap 0.167 exactly, which confirms the item separates mechanical from behavioral revenue as designed.
